# Business

## What is being sold

Not a calculator, and not advice. A **decision record**: a hashed, reproducible,
provenance-carrying document that says what you assumed and what those
assumptions implied, at the moment you committed six figures.

The simulation is the feature. The receipt is the product. The outcome dataset it
generates is the company.

## The revenue model, and the constraint on it

**The tool takes no money from lenders, universities, or agents. Ever.**

This is not positioning, it is the product. The problem being solved is that
every information source in this market is a counterparty. A tool funded by
lender referral fees is another counterparty wearing a neutral coat, and students
in this market have correctly learned to assume exactly that. The moment there is
a referral fee, the receipt is worthless — and the receipt is the only thing here
that a consultant cannot copy in a quarter.

That rules out the obvious business (lead generation, which is where every
incumbent in this space actually makes its money) and leaves:

| Line | Buyer | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Student fee | The student and their family | Small, one-time, priced in rupees |
| Institutional licence | University financial-aid offices | Honest tails beat surprise defaults |
| Employer benefit | Firms with tuition-support programs | Buyer is not the counterparty |
| Calibrated benchmarks | Accreditors, journalists, researchers | Sells the dataset, not the student |

Every recommendation the tool renders carries a machine-readable disclosure of
who pays whom. On this product that field always reads "nobody", and saying so in
a format that can be checked is the differentiator.

## The hard problem: frequency 4/10

This is a once-in-a-lifetime decision. There is no retention, no repeat purchase,
and customer acquisition cost has to be repaid in a single transaction. That is
the thesis risk, and it is worth more attention than the modelling.

Three things work against it:

**The buyer is often not the student.** Parents and co-signers are the ones
carrying the unbounded downside, and they are the ones the receipt is built for.
Institutions and employers buy on a recurring basis by definition.

**The receipt is shareable at the exact moment peers are deciding.** Applications
cluster by cohort. A document circulating in a WhatsApp group of forty people
applying in the same cycle is distribution that costs nothing.

**The decision has a long tail of sub-decisions.** Which offer to accept, whether
to take the collateralized loan, whether to defer a cycle, whether to accept the
assistantship at the lower-ranked school. That is a season of engagement, not a
single query.

## The moat

Year one, this is a smart wrapper over public data. Anyone can build it. The
public sources are public.

Year three, it is the only place where **realized outcomes are joined to the
decisions that produced them** — because the receipt gives a credible, non-creepy
reason to reopen the conversation at 18 and 36 months, and because nobody else in
the market can ask that question without their answer being suspect. A consultant
asking "how did it go?" is asking for a testimonial. A tool that has your hashed
assumptions on file is asking to be corrected.

That dataset is what makes the tail probabilities trustworthy, and trustworthy
tail probabilities are the only thing anyone is actually paying for.

## The extension the dataset unlocks

Once tail probabilities are calibrated well enough to price, they can be
**priced**. An income-contingent rider that caps repayment when
destination-country employment does not materialize converts an unbounded tail
into a premium — which is the thing that actually eliminates the anxiety, rather
than merely quantifying it.

Regulated, capital-intensive, and firmly not the MVP. But it is the reason the
calibration work is worth doing properly rather than approximately.

## MVP and the kill criterion

One corridor: **India → US, MS in CS/EE.** Ingest one fiscal year of LCA data and
the Scorecard CIP join, hard-code twenty target institutions, ship the simulator
and the receipt.

**The metric that decides it: do students forward the receipt to a parent or
co-signer?** Not sessions, not sign-ups. If they do not, the anxiety was not the
bottleneck, the receipt is not the artifact, and the thesis is wrong. Six weeks
should be enough to find out.

## What would kill it

- **Frequency.** Covered above. The most likely cause of death.
- **The stay-branch probability is the whole ballgame.** If sponsorship and
  lottery rates cannot be calibrated credibly, every downstream number is
  decoration.
- **Regulatory drift.** Immigration rules can change faster than the model.
  Receipts must be re-runnable against a stated regime, which is why the
  immigration regime is a first-class input rather than a constant.
- **Being right and unwelcome.** A tool whose honest answer is often "the tail is
  worse than you were told" is selling a conclusion nobody wants at the moment
  they are most emotionally committed. That is a real go-to-market problem and
  softening the answer would destroy the only thing worth buying.
